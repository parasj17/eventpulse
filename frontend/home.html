What it does

From measuring crowds to moving them

Most tools tell you a gate is jammed. EventPulse works out what to do about it, and checks the answer before you commit.

See

3D city digital twin

A live 3D map of the city with pressure heat, transport load, forecasts, and crowds at places nobody mapped, like a packed metro station, from attendees who choose to share their location anonymously.

Protect

Gates that never overflow

A gate turns amber at 75% and closes at 95%, and its overflow is sent to the gates with room. The venue map shows the redirected crowd, and points anyone in a packed block to the nearest gate.

How it works

Sense, predict, redirect

Three steps, repeated every few minutes while an event runs.

Sense

Occupancy, gate queues, transport load, live weather and public reports are read per zone and per gate, and turned into a live picture of the city.

Predict

A forecasting model looks six hours ahead, and the weather twin simulates what rain, heat or a surge would do, 400 times over, to show how sure it is.

Redirect

Full gates close at 95% and their crowd is sent where there's room. Attendees get an email and a live page with their best gate; organisers get the plan and the controls.

About us

Cities have spare capacity. It’s just in the wrong place.

EventPulse started from a simple observation about mega-events: the crowd problem is rarely a lack of space. It’s that everyone is told the same thing at the same moment — use the main gate, take the nearest metro — so the one obvious option is overwhelmed while others sit half empty.

We build tools that forecast that pressure early and then actively rebalance it: per gate inside a venue, per link across the transport network, and per neighbourhood across the city. The aim is the shortest safe exit for the whole crowd, not just the fastest route for one person.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Do I need to install an app?

No. Attendees get a link that opens in any browser and updates on its own.

How do I get started as an attendee?

Create an attendee account with your email, then register for the event you're going to. We email you a personal link to its live status page, and your registrations are always listed when you sign in.

Is my location tracked?

The site asks your browser for your location to show what's busy near you and the closest stays. That stays on your device. Separately, you can choose to share it anonymously to help map crowds: it's reduced to an area about 150 m across, forgotten after five minutes, never tied to your account, and only shown as a count once three or more people are in the same spot. It's off until you turn it on.

We sell tickets. Can our buyers use this?

Yes, that's what the partner API is for. An organiser creates a key in the console, your server registers each buyer, and you share their live link in your own ticket email or app. See the developer guide.

Will I get an email?

Yes. When you register, we email the address you signed up with: when the gates open, when the event starts and ends, and a button that opens your live page in your browser. If it isn't in your inbox, check spam.

Can I ask questions?

Yes. Attendees can ask EventPulse when to arrive, which gate to use, the best way out, the weather at the venue or where to stay. Organisers can ask about gates, crowds, alerts and what-ifs. Answers come from the live data, with our own model, aligned on crowd operations through Nugen, alongside.

Who can use the organiser console?

Only signed-in organisers, on their own separate sign-in. Attendee accounts can't open it, and changing gates or uploading floor plans always needs an organiser sign-in.

Is the data real?

Partly. Events, zones and crowds are simulated, so the numbers show how the system behaves rather than a real city. The weather, public posts and news are live.
